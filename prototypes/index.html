Three ways to keep what you learn

Each prototype covers the same screens: Connect, Today, Log a learning (full and quick), Library, Review (recall and weekly) and History. Use the bar at the top of each one to jump between screens. The small file notes show which file in your private repo each action writes; turn them off with Notes. Everything you see is made-up sample data, and nothing is saved.

Direction A

Reading notebook

laptop-friendlyeverything on one page

A printed reading log on screen. The library sits in a left rail, the day's page shows every field at once, and connections, the quote and due follow-ups sit in the margin.

  • Best for reading a whole book's notes back
  • You see the full entry while you write it
  • Slower on a phone: lots of fields, lots of scrolling

Direction B

Guided capture

phone-firstbest for the habit

One question per screen with a progress bar, like a short conversation. About 3 minutes for a full entry, 20 seconds in quick mode. Recall and the weekly review are short step flows too.

  • Lowest effort on a tired evening
  • Big tap targets, one thumb
  • Hard to see the whole entry while you write it
Open prototype B →
Direction C

Knowledge cards

review-firstideas as cards

Every idea becomes a card. Today is a queue: due follow-ups, then recall cards you flip and rate. The library is a shelf of sources, and each opens to its cards.

  • Makes the spaced review feel natural
  • Ideas from different books sit side by side
  • A book's story reads less well as loose cards

My recommendation: mix them

  1. Log a learning and Quick note from B. One question per screen is what keeps a phone habit going.
  2. Today and Review from C. A short queue of due cards (follow-ups first, then up to 5 recall cards) with tap-to-reveal.
  3. Library from A on wide screens (rail + full page of a book's notes), falling back to B's list and source sheet on the phone.
  4. History from B, with C's search across every card.
